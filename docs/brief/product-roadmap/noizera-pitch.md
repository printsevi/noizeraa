# Noizera

**Know what to release before you release it.**

MAKE → TEST → DECIDE → RELEASE

Investment-style pitch — v3.0, September 2026

---

## 0. One paragraph

Independent artists release blind. They have three mixes, a group chat full of polite friends, and no way to tell which version works or whether the track is ready. Noizera lets an artist test unreleased music with real listeners — their own, and a listener network drawn from a three-year-old music-discovery audience — and turn the responses into a release decision. Several products now do structured feedback on unreleased music; one pays $1.50 per review to assemble its listener panel. Noizera's listeners are already there, already interested in emerging music, and cost nothing to reach. That is the asset the company is built on, and the software exists to make it useful.

**The wedge:** an artist can get 30 real listeners on an unreleased track tomorrow, for free.
**The moat:** a compounding loop of audience, taste data and editorial credibility — not a feature.
**The business:** artists pay for access to listeners they don't have; managers and labels pay for roster tooling.

---

## 1. What changed in this version, and why

v2.0 claimed that structured version testing with a trusted panel was unbuilt. That was verified as false in September 2026:

- **Unreleased** ships Circles (invite-only groups with timestamped comments, ratings, and a vote on whether a song is ready for release), version control, completion and replay analytics, an AI Release Readiness Score, and direct-to-fan sales.
- **Carbon** sells artists a blind panel of 20 anonymous listeners and pays curators $1.50 per review, capped at 1,000 curators, currently waitlisted.
- **MixTester, SoundCheck and Trackspace** all ship some combination of blind A/B comparison, randomised order, timestamped feedback and voting.

**Two previous versions of this document each claimed an empty category and were wrong within a week.** The lesson is structural: "nobody has built this" is a claim that dies on contact with a competent search, and a pitch resting on it is fragile by construction. v3.0 does not make that claim anywhere. The defence is a compounding asset, not a missing feature.

**What this does not change:** the problem is real and now independently corroborated. Four companies building the same thing is evidence of demand, not absence of opportunity. What it changes is *why Noizera wins*, which is no longer the software.

## 2. The moat, stated honestly

Not "an uncopyable audience" — that was overclaiming too. Anyone can start an Instagram account. The defence is that three specific things reinforce each other and take years to accumulate together:

```
        AUDIENCE
   (people who follow you
    to discover new music)
            ↓
      TASTE DATA
   (what they respond to,
    across many artists)
            ↓
      CREDIBILITY
   (results worth trusting;
    features worth getting)
            ↓
      MORE ARTISTS
            ↓
   MORE INTERESTING MUSIC
            ↓
      MORE LISTENERS ──┐
            ↑          │
            └──────────┘
```

### The cost advantage this creates

Carbon pays $1.50 per review. A 20-person panel costs them $30 in listener acquisition before any margin, which sets a floor under their artist pricing and caps how generous their free tier can ever be.

Your listeners came for music discovery and will listen to unreleased tracks from emerging artists because **that is the thing they already opted into.** Panel supply costs you nothing but editorial effort you were spending anyway.

That is a durable structural advantage, and it is the only one in this document that a well-funded competitor cannot simply outbuild.

### The geographic gap

Unreleased prices in Naira and its visible catalog is Nigerian. Carbon is US-oriented. None of the verified competitors has a foothold in the European independent and electronic scene. Brussels and Benelux is a defensible starting territory — not forever, but long enough to matter.

### What the moat is not
It is not the A/B mechanic, the vault, the version control, the analytics, or the public card. All of those are table stakes, shipped by others, and are treated as such throughout this document.

## 3. Target customer

### Layer 1 — Free: emerging artists
**Purpose: supply, content, network density. Not revenue.**
1–6 years releasing, 500–50,000 monthly listeners, multiple versions of everything, decides what to release by asking a group chat. Has 20–2,000 people who genuinely care — enough for a panel, not enough for a fanbase. **Willingness to pay: low. The model must not depend on it.**

### Layer 2 — Paying: managers, micro-labels, serious artists
**Purpose: revenue.**
2–8 artists or a 5–40 track catalog, running the same "is it ready" question across a roster with no tooling. Multiple seats, annual budgets, lower churn. **This is where the business lives.**

### Layer 3 — Listeners
**Purpose: the asset itself.**
Existing Instagram followers who opt into hearing unreleased music early. Unpaid, because they came for discovery. Segmented by role (§5.3). Their accumulated response data is the taste graph.

### Layer 4 — Industry
**Purpose: year-two optionality.** A&R, DJs, venues. Acquired free as a side effect. Not courted in year one.

## 4. Problem

1. **"Which version?"** Three mixes, no way to compare. The decision gets made by whoever spoke last.
2. **"Is it ready?"** Friends say "sounds great." There is no honest signal and no memory of who said what.
3. **"Who would I even ask?"** The artist with 400 listeners cannot assemble a panel. This is the constraint every competitor hits and the one Noizera is uniquely positioned to solve.
4. **"And I still don't know who it's for."** Preference without segmentation is noise: knowing that DJs and casual listeners disagreed is more actionable than a single percentage.

**Market context, stated carefully.** AI-generated tracks exceeded 50% of daily uploads at Deezer in June 2026 (~90,000/day, up from 44% in April) while accounting for 1–3% of streams, 85% of that flagged as fraudulent. This does not prove artists need version testing — that inference was overstated in v2.0. What it supports is narrower and sturdier: **music creation and distribution are getting cheaper and more abundant, which raises the value of scarce human attention and useful feedback.** Noizera is built around that scarce resource.

## 5. Product

### 5.1 The artist side — submit
Upload versions. Choose what you're actually asking: *which mix is stronger · is the hook memorable · would you save this · does it feel ready · who is this for.* Choose the audience: your own listeners, or Noizera's.

### 5.2 The listener side — listen
Blind, order-rotated comparison. Timestamped reactions rather than a star rating. One forced comparative choice. One free-text line. Under three minutes, on a phone, no account friction beyond an email.

### 5.3 Listener roles (not just "fans")
Fan feedback is biased by definition — that is both its value and its limit. Listeners self-declare a role, and results are segmented by it:

| Role | What they tell you |
|---|---|
| **Fan** | Will your existing audience like it |
| **Peer** | How it sits against similar music |
| **Producer** | Craft, mix, arrangement problems |
| **DJ** | Club utility, energy, mix-ability |
| **Curator** | Playlist and editorial fit |
| **Casual** | How a normal listener reacts cold |

### 5.4 The decision layer — signal, with honest limits
The output is a read, not a verdict:

> **Mix B preferred, 31 of 40 (78%).** Strong preference within this panel.
> Producers split evenly; DJs preferred A (7 of 10).
> Attention drops on A at 0:20 — intro length is the likely cause.
> 18 of 40 listened twice. That's the strongest positive signal in this test.
> *This is a panel of 40 self-selected listeners. It is directional, not representative.*

**Statistical honesty is a product principle, not a disclaimer.** A panel of 20–50 cannot support population claims. Noizera reports what its listeners did, names the panel size, and never converts a preference split into a truth claim. This is also the sharpest available contrast with Unreleased's "AI Release Readiness Score" — a machine-generated number carrying an authority it cannot possibly have. Being the tool that refuses to fake certainty is a real position, and an experienced A&R or researcher will notice.

## 7. MVP — a two-week pilot, then nine weeks of build

**This is the most important change in v3.0.** The nine-week scope is preserved as decided, but a manual pilot runs first.

The reasoning: if the moat is listener supply rather than software, then the thing to test is listener supply, and that needs no product. Four competitors have already validated that the software works. Nobody has validated that *your* audience will fill panels. Nine weeks of building answers the question that's already answered and delays the one that isn't.

### Weeks 1–2: the manual pilot
No code. A form, a shared audio player, an email list.
- 20 artists from the existing community, each submitting two real versions of one unreleased track
- Listeners recruited by you, from Instagram, by hand
- Target: 20 artists × 20 listeners = **400 participants**
- Results compiled manually and sent back as a written read

Cost: two weekends. Value: the single unvalidated assumption in the entire thesis, answered before a line of code.

### Weeks 3–11: the build (contingent on the pilot passing §8)

| # | Feature | Stage |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | Upload, transcode, waveform, **version stacking**, projects | MAKE |
| 4–6 | Tracked share links, per-recipient analytics, **instant revocation** | TEST (industry) — parity only, Gatefolded has won this |
| 7 | **Panel creation + listener invitation + role self-declaration** | TEST ★ |
| 8 | **Blind, order-rotated comparison, loudness-normalised** | TEST ★ |
| 9 | **Timestamped reactions + one comparative choice + free text** | TEST ★ |
| 10 | **Segmented result view with stated panel size and honest confidence language** | DECIDE ★ |
| 11 | **Noizera listener pool — opt-in, matched by genre and role** | DECIDE ★ — the moat, in product form |
| 12 | Instagram-follower verification for listener onboarding | TEST |
| 13 | Public artist card + schema.org/sitemap | RELEASE — one day's work |
| 14 | Stripe billing | — |

**Deferred:** provenance, AI metadata, watermarking, release campaigns, pre-save, fan CRM surface, marketplace, in-app directory, mobile app, video.

## 8. Validation

### Gate 1 — the pilot (week 2). Everything depends on this.

| Signal | Metric | Pass |
|---|---|---|
| **Listener supply** | Participants recruited from Instagram | **≥300 of 400 target** |
| **Listener completion** | Completed the comparison | ≥60% of those who started |
| Artist activation | Submitted two real versions | ≥15 of 20 |
| **Decision impact** | Artist names a concrete change: different mix, shortened intro, different single, delayed release, **decided not to release** | **≥10 of 20 — the most important number in this document** |
| Repeat intent | Wants to test the next track | ≥12 of 20 |
| Commercial intent | Would pay for a larger or targeted panel *(asked after use)* | ≥6 of 20 |

**"Decided not to release" is the strongest possible positive result.** If an artist is grateful to be told a track isn't ready, the product has found real value. If they're disappointed, it's a toy.

**Fail → do not build.** The correct response is not a smaller MVP; it's reconsidering whether Noizera is a listener research network, an artist SaaS, or an Instagram media business with software attached. That question stays genuinely open until week 2.

### Gate 2 — month 4
Artists won't pay → 300 signups, fewer than 5 paying → abandon the artist tier, pivot to Layer 2, or stop.

## 9. Pricing

**Charge for audience access, not software access.** This follows directly from the moat and is the clearest monetisation idea to come out of the review process.

| Tier | Price | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| **Free** | €0 | Unlimited testing **with your own listeners**, versions, private sharing, public card, 1 Noizera panel to see what it's worth — **2GB storage, 20 tracks (soft anti-spam cap)** |
| **Artist** | €15/mo | Unlimited **Noizera panels** (up to 30 listeners), role segmentation, panel history across releases, own branding — **50GB storage, unlimited tracks** |
| **Pro** | €29/mo | Larger panels (up to 75), genre and role targeting, cross-release comparison, 2 seats, custom domain — **150GB storage, unlimited tracks** |
| **Team / Label** | €79/mo + €10/seat | 5 seats, roster analytics, private panels, custom questions, cross-artist comparison — **500GB pooled, +50GB/seat** |
| **Research** | €300–2,000 per project | Commissioned studies: *test these 10 tracks with 300 European electronic listeners.* **Requires a listener network of real scale — year two at the earliest.** |

The conversion moment is precise: the artist runs their one free Noizera panel, sees responses from 30 people who aren't their friends, and wants that again. Software access stays free forever; listener access is the product.

**Storage caps are set in bytes, not track count.** Track count varies too much by format and length to bound cost on its own — a "20 track" limit means nothing if some artists upload MP3s and others upload multitrack WAV stems. Bytes are the actual cost driver, so bytes are what's capped; track counts above are soft anti-spam limits on the free tier only, not cost controls. Uploads are restricted to audio formats — no format is disallowed on paid tiers, since a vault that can't hold a real WAV master isn't credible as the artist's actual delivery point to labels and press. The cap is enforced after upload completes (uploads go direct-to-bucket via presigned URL, so the real byte count isn't known until the object exists) via a completion callback that reads the object's actual size and rejects or deletes on overage, rather than trusting a client-declared size before the transfer.

Label pricing is set at €79 rather than the €99–299 the review proposed. Micro-labels won't pay premium rates for an unproven panel product, and pricing can rise once the network has density.

## 10. Unit economics

| | Free | Artist (€15) | Team (€79) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Storage | €0.01 | €0.23 | €2.25 |
| Transcode | €0.02 | €0.05 | €0.30 |
| Bandwidth (zero-egress storage) | €0 | €0 | €0 |
| Email (panel invitations) | €0.02 | €0.06 | €0.20 |
| **Listener incentives** | **€0** | **€0** | **€0** |
| Payment fees | — | €0.48 | €1.44 |
| **COGS** | **~€0.05** | **~€0.82** | **~€4.19** |
| **Gross margin** | — | **95%** | **95%** |

Fixed infrastructure €50–70/month. **Break-even at 6 paying artists.**

**These numbers assume typical usage, not the cap.** The caps set in §9 exist to bound the worst case, so it's worth showing that case explicitly rather than just asserting it's safe:

| | Free (2GB cap) | Artist (50GB cap) | Team (500GB cap) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Storage if every user maxes out their cap | €0.03 | €0.75 | €7.50 |
| Revenue | €0 | €15 | €79+ |
| **Margin, even at full cap** | — | **95%** | **~90%** |

Even if every paying user fills their storage to the limit every month, margin barely moves. This is what makes byte-based caps the right control: the ceiling can be set generously enough to hold real WAV masters and stems without ever threatening the unit economics — track-count limits couldn't offer that guarantee, since they don't actually bound bytes.

**The line that matters is the zero.** Carbon's equivalent row is $30 per 20-person panel. Every pricing, free-tier and margin decision in this document descends from that difference, and it is the clearest quantitative expression of the moat.

**LTV:** Artist ≈ €200 at 7% monthly churn. Team ≈ €2,400 at 3%. One label seat is worth twelve solo artists.

**Listener churn is the hidden risk.** Unpaid listeners are free but not infinitely patient. Cap participation requests per listener per month, make results visible to them ("the artist chose mix B — here's the finished track"), and treat the listener pool as a community to be maintained, not a resource to be mined. A burned-out pool is an unrecoverable failure.

## 12. Risks and kill criteria

| # | Risk | Severity | Kill criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Listeners won't participate.** The entire thesis. | **Critical** | **§8 Gate 1: under 300 participants → do not build** |
| 2 | **Listener pool burns out.** Free supply is not infinite. | **Critical** | Repeat participation below 30% by month 6 → the network model fails; fall back to artist-brings-own-panel, which is a commodity product |
| 3 | Artists won't pay | Critical | 300 signups, <5 paying by month 4 |
| 4 | **A funded competitor copies the free-listener model.** Carbon could stop paying; Unreleased could add a pool. | High | If one does and it works, the advantage is editorial credibility alone — reassess immediately |
| 5 | **Panels too small to mean anything.** | High | Median panel under 15 by month 6 → output is astrology, cut it |
| 6 | Artists distrust results from strangers rather than fans | Medium | Role segmentation is the mitigation; if artists consistently ignore non-fan segments, simplify |
| 7 | Founder bandwidth vs nobrief | High | Neither product has a paying user by month 6 → kill one |
| 8 | Instagram dependency | High | Reach down >60% for two months → rebuild acquisition first |
| 9 | Four-stage framework becomes four products | High | Any work on RELEASE/GROW before Gate 2 passes → stop |
| 10 | Storage abuse, DMCA, GDPR on listener data | Medium | Caps, EU hosting, consent, export and deletion from day one |

## 13. 12-month roadmap

**Weeks 1–2 — Pilot.** 20 artists, 400 listeners, no code. Gate 1.

**Q1, months 1–3 — Build the wedge.** Nine-week build (contingent). Placements live from week 1. Target 150 signups, 5 paying.

**Q2, months 4–6 — Find the payer.** Team seats, roster analytics, listener pool growth to 500+. Outreach to 30 Benelux managers and micro-labels. Target 500 signups, 25 paying, 3 label accounts, €600 MRR. Gate 2.

**Q3, months 7–9 — Deepen the network.** Panel history, cross-release comparison, listener retention mechanics (results visibility, participation caps, recognition). Listener pool to 1,000 — the precondition for rung 3. Minimum release-page surface, nothing more. Target 1,000 signups, 45 paying, €1,200 MRR.

**Q4, months 10–12 — Compound or consolidate.**
*Branch A* — network working, labels paying: begin rung 2, design rung 3 research offering on real segmentation data.
*Branch B* — panels work, network doesn't: artist-brings-own-panel is a commodity; consolidate on labels and placements, run profitably alongside nobrief.
*Branch C* — Gate 1 caught it in week 2 and none of this was built.

## 14. Why this, and why now

**Why this:** four companies are building structured feedback on unreleased music, which means the problem is real. Every one of them is constrained by the same thing — where do the listeners come from. One is paying $1.50 a head to solve it. You have an audience that already listens to unreleased music from emerging artists for free, because that is what they followed you for. The software is a commodity; the supply is not.

**Why now:** creation and distribution got cheap and abundant. Human attention and useful feedback got scarce. The tools are being built right now by several teams at once, and the one that wins will be the one that solves listener supply.

**Why you:** three years of exactly the right audience, an existing registered artist base to DM this week, editorial credibility that makes a panel result believable, and a pilot you can run in two weekends for the cost of your time. Competitors have better engineering. None of them can put 400 listeners on unreleased tracks by the end of the month.
